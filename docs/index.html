    Method and caveats
    • Wikipedia Current Events (default source): the leaf bullet items on each day's Portal:Current events page, an editorially curated list of the day's important news (~15–40 items/day). Share = AI items ÷ all items, pooled over the window. Global, event-shaped, volunteer-edited; it under-counts slow-burn debate relative to US front pages, so its Covid curve is steep and its AI level low. Compare shapes and multiples as well as levels.
    • GDELT TV: percent of 15-second clips on CNN, Fox News and MSNBC mentioning the query (station average), from the Internet Archive TV News archive. Cable news only covers the top few stories, so this is a prominence measure. GDELT DOC: percent of all US English online articles matching the query, a volume measure. Both appear in the source menu only when the daily job has managed to fetch them; GDELT throttles aggressively.
    • Classifier: one regular expression per topic, applied to the item text plus its parent bullets (the topic often sits in the parent, e.g. “COVID-19 pandemic › …”). Tuned for precision: AI counts only as a capitalized token; named labs, models, people and concepts are listed explicitly. Every tagged item is in data/wiki_items.jsonl for audit.
    • Matching: the first day in the Covid window whose windowed share is ≥ AI's current share. “Multiple of own baseline” divides AI by its 2017 mean and Covid by its Jan–Nov 2019 mean first, since AI had a nonzero baseline and Covid had none.
    • Structural difference: Covid was one event with an exponential ramp over ~8 weeks and direct harm to voters; AI is a slow burn with spikes. Level-matching says how loud AI is relative to a known moment, not that politics will follow the same clock.
